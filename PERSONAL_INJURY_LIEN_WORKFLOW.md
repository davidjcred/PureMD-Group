# Personal Injury Medical Lien and LOP Management Protocols

Managing medical accounts for personal injury cases involves patient care under a Letter of Protection (LOP) or medical lien rather than standard commercial insurance billing. Utilizing specialized [personal injury medical billing services](https://puremdgroup.com/solutions/personal-injury/) ensures proper case tracking, attorney coordination, and timely balance recovery. This document outlines standard operating procedures for tracking and recovering these balances.

## Common Risks in Personal Injury Billing

1. **Untracked Cases:** Treatment continues without regular status checks on the underlying legal claim.
2. **Policy Limit Caps:** Medical bills accumulate beyond the third-party bodily injury policy limits.
3. **Settlement Deductions:** Law firms attempt to negotiate large reductions at settlement without providing clear ledger audits.

## Operating Protocol for Practices

### Step 1: Lien Agreement and LOP Intake
Every personal injury patient treating on a lien must have a signed tripartite lien agreement before treatment begins.
* The agreement must be signed by the patient, the treating physician, and the patient's personal injury attorney.
* The lien must explicitly give the provider a direct interest in any settlement, judgment, or verdict proceeds under California Civil Code Section 3045.1.

### Step 2: Mid-Treatment Audit Points
Do not wait until a case settles to review the account balance.
* Set internal account milestones (for example, when total charges reach $5,000, $10,000, and $25,000).
* Request periodic case status updates from the attorney's office to confirm insurance policy limits and liability acceptance.
* Keep clinical notes up to date, as missing records delay final settlement distributions.

### Step 3: Pre-Settlement Demands and Negotiation
When the attorney requests a final medical ledger to prepare a settlement demand:
* Issue a certified, itemized bill listing all dates of service, CPT codes, and total accrued charges.
* Verify whether any health insurance or MedPay coverage paid a portion of the bill.
* Require written notice from the attorney prior to any settlement distribution.

### Step 4: Final Lien Resolution
Upon case resolution, review proposed settlement distribution sheets carefully.
* If a reduction is requested, evaluate the total policy limit, attorney fees, and net recovery to the patient.
* Ensure all compromise agreements are documented in writing before releasing the lien.
