# California Workers' Compensation Medical Billing and Lien Guide

This guide breaks down the day-to-day procedures for handling contested or delayed [workers' compensation claims in California](https://puremdgroup.com/solutions/workers-compensation/). It is maintained by the billing team at PureMD Group for practice managers, billing staff, and collection specialists.

## The Reality of California Workers' Comp Billing

Treating injured workers under California Labor Code Section 4600 should be straightforward, but claims regularly stall. Payers often cut reimbursements below the Official Medical Fee Schedule (OMFS) rate or deny coverage altogether using Utilization Review (UR) arguments.

When insurance carriers fail to pay, medical practices are left carrying aged accounts receivable. Recovering these funds requires a clean paper trail, strict adherence to state filing deadlines, and knowing when to convert a unpaid claim into a formal lien.

## Step-by-Step Recovery Workflow

### 1. Initial Intake and Coverage Verification
Before providing non-emergency care, verify that the employer has an active workers' compensation carrier and an open claim number.
* Confirm if the employer belongs to a Medical Provider Network (MPN).
* Collect the claim adjuster details, insurance carrier name, and injury date.
* Document the initial treatment authorization or Request for Authorization (RFA) submission.

### 2. Billing According to the OMFS
California uses a Relative Value Unit (RVU) system mapped to CPT codes, modified by conversion factors that update periodically.
* Submit original bills on standard CMS-1500 forms within 12 months of the date of service.
* Include detailed clinical reports (such as the Doctor's First Report of Occupational Injury or PR-2 progress reports).
* Apply accurate CPT modifiers (like Modifier 25 for significant, separately identifiable evaluation and management services).

### 3. Handling Short Payments and EOB Discrepancies
When an Explanation of Review (EOR) comes back with a payment reduction:
* Review the specific denial or reduction code.
* If the payer claims the fee exceeds the OMFS allowance, run a line-item audit against current Title 8 California Code of Regulations rates.
* File a Second Review request within 90 days of receiving the EOR if the payment was improperly reduced.

### 4. Filing a Notice and Request for Allowance of Lien
If the carrier disputes liability or ignores the Second Review request, the bill must be protected by filing a lien with the Workers' Compensation Appeals Board (WCAB).
* File Form DWC-WC-1 (Notice and Request for Allowance of Lien).
* Pay the mandatory state filing fee ($150 under current DWC rules).
* Serve a copy of the lien and proof of service on the injured worker, the employer, the insurance carrier, and their attorneys.

## Essential DWC Forms Quick Reference

| Form Number | Form Name | Primary Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| DWC-1 | Workers' Compensation Claim Form | Initiates the formal injury claim with the employer |
| DWC PR-2 | Primary Treating Physician's Progress Report | Supports ongoing medical necessity and bill submission |
| WCAB Form 6 | Notice and Request for Allowance of Lien | Protects unpaid medical balances at the WCAB |
| DWC-CA 10250.1 | Declaration of Readiness to Proceed | Requests a Lien Conference or hearing before a judge |
